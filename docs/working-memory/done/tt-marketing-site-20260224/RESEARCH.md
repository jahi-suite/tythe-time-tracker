# R — Research & Reason

## Who is the user?

- **Venue managers** at barns, wedding venues, boutique hotels
- **Small hospitality business owners** — often wearing multiple hats (events, staff, finances)
- People who may not be tech-savvy but are drowning in admin
- Often creative, entrepreneurial, juggling events and guest experience
- They care about their brand, their team, and getting payroll right

## What's their pain point?

- **Paper timesheets** in a drawer, scattered, lost
- **Chasing staff** for signed timesheets at month-end
- **Manual rate calculations** — standard vs enhanced (night), supervisor rates
- **Excel spreadsheets** that get corrupted, lost, or out of sync
- **No audit trail** when disputes arise ("I never approved that")
- **"Who worked what when?"** — constant mental overhead
- **Payroll chaos** — reconciling hours with the accountant, last-minute scrambles

## What emotion should the site trigger?

- **Relief** — "Finally, someone gets it"
- **Trust** — "This feels built for my world, not generic software"
- **Clarity** — "I understand exactly what this does"
- **Confidence** — "I can do this"

## Tone of voice

- Warm, not corporate
- Practical, not jargon-heavy
- Premium but approachable (not cheap, not enterprise)
- "We're in the same industry" — barn vibes, hospitality, not Silicon Valley

## Key insight

A venue owner doesn't want "workforce management software." They want to *stop worrying* about timesheets. The headline should land on the **outcome**, not the feature.
